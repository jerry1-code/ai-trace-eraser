# Rewrite Techniques (T1–T16)

Full definitions, rules, and before/after examples for the 16 rewriting techniques. All techniques obey the three iron rules: (1) no whole-paragraph regeneration, (2) no fabrication, (3) no word-count reduction.

---

## T1: Starter Variation

**Target dimension**: D1 (Repetitive Sentence Starters)

**Rule**: Rewrite repeated openings into varied, equally-long openings. Do not just delete them; replace with a different grammatical structure of equal or greater length.

**Before**: "This study examines the relationship between AI and innovation. This study uses a survey method. This study finds a positive correlation."

**After**: "The relationship between AI and innovation is examined in this research. A survey-based approach captures the interaction patterns. Results point to a positive correlation between the two constructs."

---

## T2: Transition De-formulaizing

**Target dimension**: D2 (Formulaic Transitions)

**Rule**: Replace formulaic connectives ("Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally", "It is worth noting that") with content-specific logical links that carry actual information about the relationship between sentences.

**Before**: "Furthermore, the coefficient is significant. Moreover, the model explains 45% of variance. Additionally, H1 is supported."

**After**: "The coefficient reaches significance at p < 0.01. Variance explained by the model is 45%, as shown in Table 3. This result supports H1."

---

## T3: Sentence Length Variation

**Target dimension**: D4 (Uniform Sentence Length and Rhythm)

**Rule**: Break uniform sentence-length patterns by splitting long sentences and varying clause structure. Split ONLY where both parts are complete sentences. Never merge sentences.

**Before**: "The results indicate that the model fits well and all paths are significant and the hypotheses are supported."

**After**: "The results indicate a good model fit. All paths are significant, and the hypotheses are supported."

---

## T4: Hedging Calibration

**Target dimension**: D6 (Overused Hedging)

**Rule**: Reduce mechanical hedge stacking ("could potentially", "may eventually", "might ultimately") to a single appropriate hedge. Never upgrade a hedge to an assertion. Never remove epistemic markers that bound the claim (Gate B protected hedges).

**Before**: "This could potentially suggest that the variable might possibly have an effect."

**After**: "This suggests that the variable has an effect."

---

## T5: Balance Disruption

**Target dimension**: D6 (Overused Hedging)

**Rule**: Break mechanical "on one hand... on the other hand" balance structures. If both sides are substantive, present them as a direct contrast rather than a symmetrical pair.

**Before**: "On one hand, AI enhances creativity. On the other hand, AI may reduce autonomy."

**After**: "AI enhances creativity, though it may reduce autonomy in certain tasks."

---

## T6: Generalization Surgery

**Target dimension**: D5 (Generic Academic Phrasing), D7 (Conclusion-Style Generalizations)

**Rule**: Replace broad, content-empty phrases with thesis-specific statements tied to actual data, variables, or findings. The replacement must be equal or greater length — do not just delete.

**Before**: "This study plays an important role in the evolving landscape of AI research and has important theoretical and practical significance."

**After**: "This study isolates three mediating paths between AI collaboration and innovative behavior, providing specific evidence for the self-efficacy mechanism that prior studies (Chen, 2024; Liu, 2025) hypothesised but did not test."

---

## T7: Passive Voice Control

**Target dimension**: D8 (Passive Voice and Nominalization)

**Rule**: Convert clusters of passive constructions to active voice where the agent is clear. Keep passive where the agent is genuinely unknown or where the field convention requires it.

**Before**: "The implementation of the survey was conducted and the data were analyzed using SEM."

**After**: "The research team implemented the survey and analyzed the data using SEM."

---

## T8: Concrete Anchoring

**Target dimension**: D9 (Abstract-to-Concrete Imbalance)

**Rule**: Ground abstract claims with specific data references, table numbers, variable names, or concrete examples already present in the thesis. Never invent new examples.

**Before**: "The results show that the model has good explanatory power."

**After**: "As shown in Table 4.3, the coefficient for self-efficacy is positive and significant at p < 0.01, explaining 45% of the variance in innovative behavior."

---

## T9: Citation Naturalization

**Target dimension**: D10 (Citation Pattern Regularity)

**Rule**: Vary citation placement — sometimes narrative (Author (Year) argued...), sometimes parenthetical (... finding (Author, Year)). Use citations to support specific claims, not as sentence-end decorations.

**Before**: "AI affects creativity (Smith, 2024). AI also affects motivation (Jones, 2025). AI influences performance (Brown, 2023)."

**After**: "Smith (2024) found that AI affects creativity, while Jones (2025) extended this to motivation. The effect on performance, as Brown (2023) demonstrated, depends on the task type."

---

## T10: Lexical Precision and Repetition Control

**Target dimension**: D3 (Over-Smooth Logical Flow), D11 (Lexical Flatness), D14 (AI-Like Smoothness)

**Rule**: Replace overused academic verbs and adjectives with more precise alternatives. Add visibility to the author's reasoning process — show why a step was taken, not just what was done. The added reasoning must be based on existing thesis content only.

**Before**: "This study examines the relationship. The results demonstrate significance. This is an important finding."

**After**: "This study tests the relationship because prior work (Smith, 2024) left the mediating path untested. The results, significant at p < 0.01, confirm the hypothesized mediation."

---

## T11: Abstract Word Reduction

**Target dimension**: D5 (Generic Academic Phrasing), D9 (Abstract-to-Concrete Imbalance)

**Rule**: Replace abstract, content-empty words with concrete thesis-specific terms. Keep technical terms from the discipline. Never reduce word count in the process — the replacement must be equal or greater length.

**Before**: "Various factors play a significant role in the process."

**After**: "Self-efficacy, work engagement, and creative inspiration each contribute to the innovation process, as the regression coefficients in Table 4 confirm."

---

## T12: Paragraph Architecture Rebuilding

**Target dimension**: D3 (Over-Smooth Logical Flow), D12 (Paragraph-Level Template Structure)

**Rule**: Change the paragraph's entry point and internal sentence order. Instead of the AI-typical "background → purpose → method → result → conclusion" order, start from the problem, the result, or the contrast. Every sentence must still trace to an original sentence — this is reordering, not regeneration.

**Before** (template: background→gap→purpose→method): "AI is increasingly used in workplaces. However, its effect on innovation is unclear. This study examines this relationship. A survey was conducted with 300 employees."

**After** (entry from problem): "Whether AI collaboration enhances or constrains innovation remains an open question in the current literature. A survey of 300 employees tests this relationship directly, addressing the gap that Smith (2024) and Jones (2025) identified between AI usage and creative output."

---

## T13: Section-Level Pattern Diversification

**Target dimension**: D13 (Cross-Paragraph Structural Repetition), D15 (Thesis-Section Template Dependency)

**Rule**: When neighbouring paragraphs share the same architecture, differentiate each one's entry point, internal order, and function. Do NOT merge paragraphs. Each paragraph keeps its own break. Each gets a different functional entry: one from the problem, one from the data, one from the theory, one from the contrast.

**Example**: For four hypothesis paragraphs all following "variable → mechanism → citation → hypothesis":
- Paragraph 1: enter from the cognitive mechanism
- Paragraph 2: enter from the emotional or motivational involvement
- Paragraph 3: enter from the dependent variable's problem
- Paragraph 4: enter from the organizational boundary condition

---

## T14: Methodological Template Breaking

**Target dimension**: D12 (Paragraph-Level Template Structure)

**Rule**: Break the "definition → scale source → item count" or "test purpose → threshold → result → conclusion" template by restructuring within the paragraph. Reorder information, change sentence forms, and add specificity from existing thesis content. Never compress or delete. Length must be preserved or increased.

**Before** (template: purpose→threshold→result→conclusion): "The purpose of the reliability test was to check internal consistency. The threshold is 0.7. Cronbach's alpha is 0.85. This indicates good reliability."

**After**: "Cronbach's alpha for this scale is 0.85, above the 0.7 threshold that the field treats as the minimum for internal consistency. The test was run to confirm that the adapted items, which were modified from Chen's (2024) original 12-item version, still form a coherent measure after the AI-collaboration context changes."

---

## T15: Sequence Reordering

**Target dimension**: D12 (Paragraph-Level Template Structure), D15 (Thesis-Section Template Dependency)

**Rule**: Reorder the reporting sequence within a paragraph. Move key results before generic explanations. Change what comes first, second, and third — but keep all information and never change the paragraph's position in the section.

**Before**: "The test purpose was to verify validity. The threshold is 0.5. AVE is 0.62. This confirms convergent validity."

**After**: "AVE for this construct is 0.62, confirming convergent validity above the 0.5 threshold. The test verifies that the measurement items, adapted from the original scale, capture the intended construct in the AI-collaboration context."

---

## T16: Source-Extracted Researcher-Trace Relocation

**Target dimension**: D3 (Over-Smooth Logical Flow), D9 (Abstract-to-Concrete Imbalance), D14 (AI-Like Smoothness), D15 (Thesis-Section Template Dependency)

**Rule**: Relocate or lightly rephrase researcher-side material (research decisions, limitations, boundary conditions, scope narrowing, process narration) that ALREADY EXISTS elsewhere in the thesis. This is extraction and relocation ONLY — never generation of new researcher voice.

**Mandatory source annotation** (anti-fabrication hard rule): every T16 relocation MUST be logged with its source:

```text
Source Trace:
- Operation type: T16 relocation
- Original thesis source location (chapter/section/paragraph):
- Exact source wording:
- Revised / inserted wording:
- Relation to original: relocated / lightly rephrased
- Protected elements affected: None
- New unsupported content added: No
```

If no existing source can be named, do NOT add the sentence. Write "Author confirmation needed" instead.

**Before** (smooth, no friction): "AI collaboration enhances employee innovation. The mechanism operates through self-efficacy. H1 is proposed."

**After** (with relocated researcher trace): "AI collaboration enhances employee innovation, though the review in Section 2.3 noted that this effect depends on task autonomy — a boundary this thesis tests in Chapter 5. The mechanism operates through self-efficacy, which Bandura (1997) defined as belief in one's capability to organize and execute actions. H1 is proposed."

*Source trace: the boundary condition sentence was relocated from the literature review discussion of task autonomy in Section 2.3, Paragraph 4.*

---

## Usage Rules

- Always start at the lightest intensity (T1–T10) and escalate only when needed.
- T16 is NOT a default tool — use only when suitable researcher-side material already exists.
- Never use T16 to insert fabricated researcher feelings, quotes, or observations.
- Every technique must preserve word count (Principle 4) and all protected elements (Gate B).
- Techniques may be combined within a single paragraph — e.g. T1 + T3 + T8.
